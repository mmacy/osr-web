# Client facts

## Client gotchas

- The browser aggressively memory-caches `app.js`. `index.html` references it as `app.js?v=N`. **Bump N whenever you edit app.js**, or your change silently won't load. `scripts/check_app_version.py` enforces the bump in CI on every pull request: it fails a pull request whose `static/app.js` differs from the base branch while the version query does not.
- Set `<select>` values via `select.value = x` after appending options. Setting `option.selected` on detached options doesn't stick.
- Sessions are in-memory: restarting uvicorn orphans every client, and `boot()` then lands on the front door, offering the newest save. Save first (`POST .../save`) when you test restarts.
- Esc walks the screens. In game, each press closes one thing (the sheet, then the journal, then the map), and with none open it opens the menu. On an overlay screen it goes back one step (`goBack()`). In the wizard it's deliberately inert, so a stray press can't scrap a half-made character. The title and **the two ending screens are the floor**: `goBack()` has no branch for `gameover` or `victory` and gets none, so an unmatched screen stays put.
- **`.overlay-card` sets `margin: auto`, and it's a clip guard, not spacing.** `.overlay` centers with `align-items: center`, and a flex item taller than its scrolling container overflows *both* ends. The top of the card goes out of reach, with or without a scrollbar. Auto margins absorb the free space before alignment does, so a short card centers and a tall one pins to the scroll top. The guard lives on the shared base rule, not a per-screen modifier. The victory card (whose journal grows with the adventure) is only the first screen that can get tall.
- **The wireframe's focal length is anchored to `VP.W`, never `VP.H`. There are two knobs, and they're easy to confuse.** `drawScene`'s `f = VP.W * 0.7875` is the **zoom** knob: change the multiplier and every wall narrows or widens. `VP` (with the `#viewport` canvas attributes and `.viewport-frame`'s `aspect-ratio`, all three edited together) is the **letterbox** knob: change `VP.H` alone and the same scene is cropped or extended top and bottom, horizon still centered (`cy = VP.H / 2`). That's why letterboxing from 4:3 to 16:9 keeps `f` at 724.5 (920 × 0.7875, the same number as 690 × 1.05). Measured, the 920×518 render is pixel-for-pixel the vertically centered crop of a 920×690 one (86px off each end). Anchoring `f` to `VP.H` instead (`VP.H * 1.05`, the same 724.5 at 690) would turn a shrunken `VP.H` into a zoom-out.
- **The map and the journal are both `.scrim` overlays, only one is open at a time, and both block the exploring keys.** Opening either closes the other (one clause in each of `toggleMap`/`toggleJournal`), because both are `.scrim` at `z-index: 20` and two open at once would stack two panels. Both appear in the exploring keys' early return (`if (MAP.open || JOURNAL.open || S.pacing) return;`). The scrim swallows clicks, so a missing clause there is invisible to any click-driven check and lets `W` walk the party behind a covered screen. Both are closed by `enterGame` and `clearGameState`, the two choke points where a screen is replaced. A scrim that outlived a wipe would sit under the game-over overlay (`#overlay` is `z-index: 30`) and still be there for the next party. The journal itself has no availability gate at all. `J` works in town, in an encounter, in battle, and while exploring, the button is never hidden or disabled, and an empty journal says so in words.
- Static files are served straight from disk, so a server restart is only needed for `server/*.py` changes.

## Voice and layout

- The UI is a quiet, modern shell around an old-school game. Neutral near-black grounds and hairlines, one amber accent (light/treasure/primary action), red for danger only. Three type voices: system sans for the interface, serif (Iowan Old Style stack) for the referee's prose, mono for dice and mechanics. Pick from these tokens (`:root` in `style.css`) for new UI. Don't introduce new colors or themed decoration.
- The layout is text-first. The transcript owns the main column in the dungeon modes. In town the stage splits between the town board (description, services, rumors, and a card grid where the quests card comes before the commerce cards) and the transcript, which stays live beside it. The wireframe viewport, actions, party rows, and mode panels live in the left rail in every mode. In town the rail's `#context` is empty. While exploring, `#context` contains the "This area" card, then the quests card, then the cast card ("Cast a spell": caster, spell, member target). **That order is measured, not aesthetic, and so is the viewport's shape.** At the reference viewport (1440×900, the one `tests/screenshots/conftest.py` sets) the rail gets 848px of visible height (`clientHeight`). Its scrollbar is deliberately near-invisible (`scrollbar-width: thin` over a transparent track), so nothing announces that the rest is below the fold. All the numbers below are rail-box-relative: the rail's own `getBoundingClientRect().top` is the origin, which is what makes that 848 the fold. All were measured on the barrow fixture (`tests/assets/barrow-adventure.json`) at quest activation, exploring, on a featureless cell. Under a 4:3 frame the fixed furniture runs to 700.3 of the 848: the viewport at 12.0 to 338.3 (**326.3px**), the action bar at 350.3 to 460.8, and the party rows at 472.8 to 700.3. That puts the third card down off screen, whichever card it is. With the quests card last, it runs 867.5 to 1010.9 and no part of it is visible. Moving it ahead of the cast card (712.3 to 855.7, every line of content visible) only hands the problem to the cast card, at 867.7 to 1010.9. **The fix is in the fixed furniture, and the viewport is the piece that shrinks: the wireframe is letterboxed to 920×518 (16:9) rather than 4:3, which yields 81px, and nothing else moves.** The wireframe's information is in the horizon band (walls, doors, the vanishing point). What a 4:3 frame spends its extra height on is blank near-floor and near-ceiling fill, so the crop loses nothing the wireframe uses, and the wireframe stays the "small, honest window" it is meant to be. At 16:9, same fixture, same state, same convention: viewport 12.0 to 256.9 (**244.9px**), action bar 268.9 to 379.4, party rows 391.4 to 618.9, the quests card 630.9 to 774.3 **entire**, and the cast card 786.3 to 929.6 with its heading and caster select on screen (that select's last pixel lands at 847.8 against the 848 fold) and its spell select and Cast button below. Three residual cases are recorded rather than fixed, with the shipped default's own numbers (the Cold Vein card is ~186px against the barrow's 143: two objectives and a longer offer). On a featureless cell the Cold Vein quests card runs 630.9 to 816.8, still **entire**, and it stays entire only while the authored offer holds at two rendered lines. The cast card behind it starts at 828.8, and its heading crests the 848 fold. On a featured cell the "This area" card above it (99.4px at the crib cache, `crib-jar` in level 1 area 4) pushes the split to exactly the offer's last line. The heading, the quest's name, and the whole two-line offer stay on screen (offer bottom 846.1 against the 848 fold) while the speaker attribution (851.1) and the objectives (877.1) go under. That case is transient (the player is standing on something they're about to act on), and the charge itself is still visible. In battle the same 81px goes to the declaration panel and group chips at no cost: measured, the declaration and enemy panels end at 716.2 with 130px to spare. The rule behind the ordering: a card the player must *see* to know it exists goes above a card the player goes looking for. The exploring action bar is three fixed full-width zones: the movement cluster, the persistent verbs (Search, Find traps, Rest, Map: always present in the same order, never moving, with the auto-picked actor named on the button), and a contextual row (door, treasure, stairs, light, leave) that reserves one button row's height so verbs appearing and disappearing never reflow the rail. The automap is an overlay (M) with client-side player notes (localStorage, never the server). The journal is a second overlay (J, or the topbar button) available in every mode. The character sheet is a modal (double-click a party row) fed by the server's player-safe `sheets` extras.
